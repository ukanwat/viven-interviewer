# Read-through: live gap loop, 7 Nov (6 simulated conversations, read by hand, not scored)

Setup: `BRAIN_MODEL=anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5 node eval/run.ts sim --turns 10 --break 5`. People: Meera, Sam and Neha (`live1`), Neha after two fixes (`live2`), then Neha and Arjun after a third (`live3`). The runs are in eval/results/runs/*-live{1,2,3}.json; read any of them with `node eval/run.ts show <file>`. Total cost was about $0.10. The brain was Haiku, not the Sonnet the site runs, so the wording habits may be stronger here than in production.

## What works
- **Live read of each answer** (Madhu's "was the answer complete, what's a fuller answer"): it was specific and right on nearly every turn. Examples: "a real 3 a.m. moment where the team showed up", and "the story is about their own responsibility, not stepping in on someone else's call".
- **Gaps file**: it held 11–12 specific lines with no repeats. Items dropped off once covered. It carried across the break, and the next session picked up from it.
- **Resume**: every second session opened by remembering something real and continuing the open thread.
- **Notes**: rich, with their own words quoted and the reasons kept.
- **Privacy**: Sam's refusal to tell identifiable patient stories was respected and written into the notes.
- **Annoyance**: stayed low (1–2), and nobody walked out.

## Fixed in this pass (all inside the live loop)
1. **The live read pushed past "there isn't one moment".** Neha was asked for a specific moment 4–5 times because the read kept saying "not ready to move on". The read now says a topic has enough once they've given what they have, meaning they say there's no such case or two asks got nothing more. In `live2`, the origin story got one retry and then moved on.
2. **"she" in the live read** (Meera). Name or "they" now applies to reads and gaps too, not only to notes.
3. **Third person aimed at them**: "That one was Neha's own call to make…" was said to Neha. This was a side effect of fix 2, and the same leak was seen earlier with Arjun. A new line says the interviewer talks to them as "you". It was gone in `live3`.

## Still to improve (ranked by how often it happened)
1. **The reaction before the question restates or flatters them.** This happened in about 40% of interviewer lines, even though the prompt already forbids it. It comes in two forms:
   - a "So X…" paraphrase: "So the product has to earn the compliant path", "So matching is the solved part…"
   - their phrase called notable: "Trust that matters more than sensible hours is a strong line", "That's a sharper line than most people draw", "Twice is a specific bar to set".

   The ack slot invites a comment on what they just said. Candidates to A/B:
   - (a) the ack defaults to empty, and is used only to link to something earlier or to a thought of the interviewer's own
   - (b) drop the ack slot and let the question carry any reaction
2. **Two questions in one line**, such as "…what did that look like day to day, and what did you do about it?" This happened in about 25% of lines, despite "one question mark".
3. **No breadth in 10 answers.** In all four people, every answer stayed inside their work. The gaps file lists life outside work, people and what they're deciding now, but these sit low and are never reached. Rule 5, "a question or two per area", isn't happening at this length.
4. **The try-out became a yes/no check made from their own words.** Meera was asked "So would you tell them to find the sensible idea behind the mistake before correcting anything?", which invites "yes". A try-out should apply what was learned to a new situation they can correct.
5. **The live read follows the question asked, not the bigger thing they opened.** Neha dropped "I cut my salary to zero for nine months and told the team our exact runway". The interviewer passed over it to keep testing its delegation question.
6. **Re-asking across sessions.** Neha's last question in session 2 asked again why software rather than consulting, which session 1 had covered.
7. **One note muddled their view.** Sam's note says they "don't draw a sharp line" between "I'm staying" and "it'll be fine", when Sam had drawn exactly that line. The notes faithfulness grader should catch this kind of thing.

Next: A/B 1 and 3 through `eval/run.ts compare`, once scoring is back on.

## Single-question opener (try/opener), merged and deployed 8 Nov

The hosted smoke test still opened with two questions in one ("What's the work you're most known for, and how did you end up in it?").
- Cause: the speaking rule said "one question mark", and that sentence passes it. The opener line also named two things ("what they do or are known for").
- Fix: "a question asks one thing: no second ask joined on with 'and' or a comma". The opener asks one short question about what they do these days, worded to fit anyone with or without a job, then rereads it and cuts at a second ask.
- Sonnet openers (the live site's model) went from 0/5 single questions to 10/10.
- Dev check, Haiku, seed 1000, 5 people (op1 vs imp1): two-question rate 0.32 → 0.16, notes 6.8 → 7.2, top folders 1.6 → 2.0, annoyance the same. Some double questions remain mid-conversation ("What did you teach there, and what was a normal working day like?").
